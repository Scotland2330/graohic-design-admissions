Paper kit. Print on letter paper (cardstock for the cards). Pages: 1 table sign · 2–3 poster cards (4 found stills) · 4 caption slips · 5 facilitator sheet. Bring round color-coding dot stickers (½" and ¾"), a few black markers, and painter's tape for a "wall" of finished pieces.

Put a dot
on it.

  1. 1. CoverStick a colored dot over a face. Or every face.
  2. 2. CaptionWrite one flat, literal, slightly-too-honest line under it.
  3. 3. DoneSign it. It's art now. Take it home or add it to the wall.

After John Baldessari, who covered faces in found photos with price-tag dots so you'd look at everything else.

A 60-second design challenge

Stuck? Steal a caption.

Running the table

The pitch (10 seconds): "Want to make a piece of art in a minute? Pick a photo, cover a face with a dot, give it a caption. That's how one of the most important conceptual artists in LA worked, and it's how graphic design works too."

Keep the line moving

  • Aim for 60 seconds per visitor. Hand them a card and the sticker sheet in one motion.
  • If they freeze on the caption, hand them a slip from page 4. Done is better than perfect.
  • Have two or three people working at once; the tablet version handles one, paper handles the overflow.
  • Tape finished pieces in a grid behind the table. A growing wall draws the next crowd.

Talking points while they work

  • Covering the face makes you look at everything else: posture, gesture, the space between people. Designers steer attention for a living.
  • Baldessari worked with found film stills and press photos. Remixing existing images is art direction.
  • Picture + caption is the basic unit of graphic design. Every poster, ad and magazine spread starts there.
  • A deadpan caption changes how we read a picture. Type is never neutral.
  • Baldessari burned his early paintings in 1970 and started over. Iteration is part of studio culture.

Close

  • "If you liked making that, you'd like our first-year studio." Then hand over the program card or QR code to the inquiry form.
  • Ask permission before photographing a student's piece for social media.
